## Structural feedback

The four-paragraph shape is right, and for the first time you have faced a **two-part question** — and you mapped it correctly: Body 1 answers *why is this happening*, Body 2 answers *is it positive*. That is exactly what `contexts/05-band-6-to-7/task-response-upgrades.md` §8.5 prescribes, and it is real credit.

Three structural repairs, in value order. **First, the essay only covers half the prompt's scope.** The prompt says "at home **and** work". After the introduction, "home" never reappears: your example set is web pages, Tetris, bug-hunting, button shapes and program content — all workplace software. A robot vacuum, a dishwasher, a laundry machine — one sentence would close this. This is the scope test in `contexts/08-vocabulary-grammar/04-task-response-error-correction-bank.md` §1 row 6.

**Second, both bodies run on one idea.** Body 1 says robots are efficient at repetitive work; Body 2 says robots handling small things lets people be efficient on big things. These are cause and consequence of the same claim, so your answer to *why* and your answer to *is it positive* are the same answer twice — the W7 fault returning (§3 row 8: each body needs a distinct main idea). Give Body 1 a **cause** the second paragraph does not have: cost. Automating a routine task has become cheaper than paying a person to do it.

**Third, and worth more than any lexical fix below: the conclusion.** Twenty words, an almost word-for-word echo of your thesis, and it answers only the second question. Band 6 names this verbatim — "the conclusions drawn may be unclear, unjustified or **repetitive**." A two-part question needs a conclusion that summarises **both** answers, in fresh words, at 40–60 words (`contexts/05-band-6-to-7/paragraph-structure-band7.md` §§1, 5).

One smaller drift: `robots such as computers, smartphones or other devices` redefines the prompt's subject. A smartphone is not a robot, and once you say it is, your evidence stops being about the thing the prompt asked about (`contexts/10-task2-topic-banks/topic-03-technology.md` §Common pitfalls).
