## Structural feedback

The four-paragraph problem/solution shape is right, and answering "a possible solution" with one developed solution is exactly what the prompt asks. Three structural faults keep the response at Band 6.

First, **nothing in the essay is proved with an example**. Body 1's accident claim rests on "car accidents will increase naturally" — "naturally" is doing the work an explanation should do (more first-time, inexperienced drivers on unimproved roads is the mechanism; a named city or country would anchor it). This is the vague-support pattern in `contexts/05-band-6-to-7/band-6-common-errors.md` §D7, and it is a step backwards from W7's Korea example.

Second, **the solution only solves half the problem set**. Per `contexts/05-band-6-to-7/task-response-upgrades.md` §8.4, a problem/solution essay must show each stated problem being addressed. Your campaign → fewer cars → less traffic chain implicitly covers accidents, but fossil fuels — your other named problem, and three of Body 1's five sentences — are never mentioned again until the conclusion. One sentence closes this loop (rewrite #8).

Third, **Body 2 restates instead of developing**: "there will be less cars on roads", "there will be less traffics on road" and "instead of people driving their own vehicles each" are the same idea three times — the same repeat-instead-of-advance fault as W7's Body 2. A small related wobble: the introduction lists fuels then accidents, but the body treats accidents then fuels; matching the roadmap to the body order is a free coherence gain.
