## Structural feedback

Your introduction does the hardest thing right: it paraphrases both views and lands a position on the prompt's own terms — "I believe that parents are more responsible to their children." That is the exact failure W3 had, and it is fixed. The problem is that the essay then never returns to that position. Your conclusion — "despite the fact that teachers in schools are experts to guide children and educate how to socialize with others and become nice adults, it is easy to get outnumbered by children" — concedes the teacher case and trails off into a subordinate observation; a reader finishing here could not say which side you took. That is a textbook Band 6 match for "the conclusions drawn may be unclear." The bodies are also unbalanced in the wrong direction: 119 words for the view you *reject* against 88 for the view you *hold*, so your own argument gets the thinner treatment. Finally, Body 1's Korean certification example proves teachers are academically qualified, not that they are good at teaching social skills — the proposition you actually need. Fix the conclusion first; it is worth more than every lexical upgrade in the table below combined.

## Per-criterion justification

**Task Response — 6.0.** Both views are addressed and your position is stated in the introduction, which meets the Band 6 floor. It fails Band 7's "clear position throughout the response" because the conclusion never returns to it: "it is easy to get outnumbered by children" is an observation about teachers, not a verdict about parents. Coverage is uneven — 119 words defending the school side you reject versus 88 for your own. The certification example ("they need to graduate the school of education and get a bachlor degree, and pass the exam") proves subject qualification, not social-skills competence, so it supports a proposition you did not make. "Some might have mental health issues or even a rare disease" is a further drift: a rare disease is not a social-skills variable. This is the Band 6 pattern in `contexts/04-task2-essays/samples-band-6_0.md` §Common Patterns #7 and #8.

**Coherence and Cohesion — 6.0.** The four-paragraph shape is sound and the on-the-one-hand/on-the-other-hand pairing is used correctly. Cohesion becomes faulty at the sentence seam: "parents know their children more than anyone else, thus, they should know how to effectively guide their children" is a comma splice, the same fault as W3's "therefore some people take a different view". Reference is imprecise in three places — "that issue" in the introduction has no clear antecedent, and the two "they"s in "as a homeroom teacher, it is challenging to deal with these factors since there are so many children they have to take care of" point at different referents. Body 2 also drifts: it opens on parental knowledge but ends back on teacher overwhelm, repeating Body 1's point rather than developing your own. Band 7 needs the flexible reference described in `contexts/05-band-6-to-7/cohesion-upgrades.md`, not just accurate linkers.

**Lexical Resource — 6.0.** Two misspellings sit in one sentence — "cirtification" and "bachlor" — which is the explicit Band 6 marker "makes some errors in spelling and/or word formation". Register misfires: "juveniles" is a legal/criminological word for schoolchildren (flagged as mis-collocation in `contexts/04-task2-essays/samples-band-5_5.md` §Sample 5), "nice adults" is too informal for an academic conclusion, and "so many children" is spoken register. Collocations are near-misses rather than errors of meaning: "responsible to teach" (→ responsible *for* teaching), "educate students how to" (→ teach students how to), "graduate the school of education" (→ graduate *from*), "I am inclined to this view" (→ inclined *towards*), and "efficiently teach" where you mean **effectively** — the effect/efficient/effective confusion catalogued in `contexts/09-instructor-tips/10-common-korean-chinese-speaker-errors.md` §J. The range attempts are real, which is why this is a solid 6 and not a 5.5.

**Grammatical Range & Accuracy — 6.0 (low).** Range is genuine: concessive "Although", "even though", "despite the fact that", multiple relative clauses, and the "it is often true that" frame. Accuracy under that range is not. Only three of your thirteen sentences are error-free — "On the one hand, children can learn how to socialize in school effectively thanks to homeroom teachers.", "Moreover, everyone has different traits and personalities.", and "Some might have mental health issues or even a rare disease." — and two of those are short and simple. Band 7 requires "frequently produces error-free sentences"; three, of which two are simple, is not frequent. The errors cluster in the complex material exactly as the Band 6 descriptor predicts: "Some would argued" (modal + past participle), "experts who has cirtification" (relative-clause agreement), "teachers cannot efficiently teach every children" (number), "they had observed" (unmotivated past perfect), and the dangling modifier "as a homeroom teacher, it is challenging" — which literally says *you* are a homeroom teacher.

## Before your next essay

- **Write the conclusion first, in two sentences, before you draft the bodies.** Sentence 1 concedes the other side; sentence 2 states your verdict in your own words. Template: *"Although X, on balance Y, because Z."* This single habit is worth a half-band to you.
- **Give your own view the longer paragraph.** Target 80–110 words per body (`contexts/05-band-6-to-7/paragraph-structure-band7.md` §1). You gave the side you reject 31 more words than the side you hold.
- **Run a two-minute subject sweep.** For every clause, point at the subject and its verb. Fix "who has" → "who have" and supply the missing subject in every "it is…" sentence. This targets both of your recurring grammar patterns at once (`contexts/09-instructor-tips/10-common-korean-chinese-speaker-errors.md` §C, §K).
- **Circle every noun and ask "one or many? which one?"** — the fix for "every children", "a bachlor degree", "the school of education".
- **Add one `Adj + as + S + V` concession per essay** — e.g. *"Qualified as teachers are, they cannot replace daily parental guidance."* It is the highest GRA return per character of effort in `contexts/09-instructor-tips/07-band7-grammar-showcases.md` §4, and it fits this prompt naturally.
