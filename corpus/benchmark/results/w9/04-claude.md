## Structural feedback

Four paragraphs, correct proportions (55 / 116 / 104 / 36), a clear thesis, and — new this round — a genuine concession paragraph before the counter-argument. That architecture is Band 7 shaped.

The fault is what the two bodies are *for*. Body 1 concedes the risk and then rebuts it ("there is always a risk to ge stolen even though individuals choose not to use the sevice"). Body 2 then argues technology is beneficial — but nowhere against the data cost. `contexts/04-task2-essays/question-types-overview.md` §3 lists this under Pitfalls: "Listing more advantages than disadvantages without explaining the asymmetry — examiners want a *reason* for the verdict." Compare `contexts/08-vocabulary-grammar/04-task-response-error-correction-bank.md` §3 row 4 (generic ideas with no link back to the prompt): "people are ableto get health or legal advice from AI chatbot service" is true of AI in general, not of *paying with your data*.

## Focus areas

1. **Weigh, don't list.** Every Body 2 claim must end with a clause that compares the benefit to the data given up.
2. **Collocation precision** — *breach a database* (not "breach into"), *consult a doctor* (not "reach to"), *work efficiently* / *live comfortably* (not "live efficiently" three times).
3. **Articles and countability** — *software*, *data*, *information*, *access* are uncountable and take no *a*; `contexts/08-vocabulary-grammar/01-grammar-error-correction-bank.md` §1 row 12.
4. **A proofread sweep** (replaces the retired splice check). Six corruptions in one essay is what blocks Lexical Resource 7 *here*, even though the long-run pattern is milder than the other three.
5. **Anchor every pronoun** to the nearest matching plural noun, not to the one you mean.
