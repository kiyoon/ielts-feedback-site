## Structural feedback

The essay addresses both sides and states that the benefits outweigh the risk. Compared with W4, the same question type, the treatment is better balanced: both bodies exceed 100 words and contain a claim, explanation and example. However, the argument repeatedly changes the comparison from **providing data versus protecting it** to **using technology versus not using it**. Body 1 also relies on the unsupported absolute that all personal information is already digital, while Body 2’s AI services are not explicitly connected to the data users surrender. The conclusion maintains your direction but neither synthesises the exact trade-off nor gives “they” a clear referent. The single highest-impact fix here is rewriting Body 2 around the data → software access → time/cost benefit chain and then explicitly weighing that benefit against the additional privacy risk, not the lexical upgrades below (`contexts/05-band-6-to-7/task-response-upgrades.md`).

However, “Modern technology plays a vital role” broadens the task, and neither AI example shows why personal data must be exchanged to obtain the benefit. The verdict is therefore relevant but inadequately proved, matching Band 6’s allowance for insufficiently developed or less relevant support (`contexts/01-band-descriptors/task2-band-descriptors.md` §Band 6).

**Coherence and Cohesion — 6.0.** Risk → rebuttal → benefit → conclusion is a clear overall progression, and the paragraph lengths are balanced. Cohesion is sometimes conspicuous or faulty: “Admittedly, it is undeniable” duplicates one function, while “The reason is that,” “A classic example,” and “For these reasons” provide visible scaffolding. Repetition of “personal information,” “modern technology” and “efficiently,” plus ambiguous final “they,” prevents flexible reference and substitution (`contexts/05-band-6-to-7/cohesion-upgrades.md`).

**Lexical Resource — 6.0.** “Database,” “digital form,” “artificial intelligence,” “AI agents” and “cutting-edge software” show adequate topic range. Precision is inconsistent in “breach into,” “gain the access,” “numerous data,” “reach to a doctor” and “live a life more efficiently.” The six spelling/spacing slips are noticeable, but meaning remains generally recoverable. This fits the Band 6 risk-taker profile rather than Band 7 collocational control (`contexts/01-band-descriptors/task2-band-descriptors.md` §Band 6).

**Grammatical Range and Accuracy — 6.0.** You attempt concession, passives, relative clauses, conditionals and a participle clause: “allowing them to make whatever they want.” Complex structures are much less accurate than simple ones, as shown by “a companies’ database,” “using modern technologies allow” and the broken passive “a risk to ge stolen.” The conclusion also needs “a more significant downside,” lower-case “despite,” and deletion of the fragment “ortdra.” Errors rarely block meaning, but error-free sentences are not frequent enough for Band 7 (`contexts/05-band-6-to-7/grammar-upgrades.md`).
