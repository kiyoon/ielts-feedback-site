### Structural feedback

The four-paragraph shape is right and the position is stated in the introduction and never wobbles — that is real progress you should protect. Body 1 handles the opposing view correctly: you state it, explain it, illustrate it, and judge it in the last sentence. The weakness is what Body 1 argues *about*: it defends the value of art rather than the case for spending tax money on art, so the paragraph doesn't quite meet the proposition on the page. Body 2's core claim ("not an efficient way of spending taxes") is the right claim, but its whole evidential weight rests on one sentence about chatbots, which cannot carry an absolute like "they can be replaced by modern technologies". The conclusion repeats Body 2 in compressed form instead of pulling the two sides together. At 287 words you are below the 250-word minimum's comfort margin — you have room for roughly 30–40 more words, and the best place to spend them is a second reason inside Body 2.

### Focus areas

1. **Articles, countability and number.** Five slips in twelve sentences: "a huge financial support", "a efficient way", "contribute to **the** society", "soothe people's **mind**", "not the only **one**". Korean has no article system and no obligatory plural marking, so this collapses first under time pressure (`contexts/09-instructor-tips/10-common-korean-chinese-speaker-errors.md` §A2, §B1; `contexts/09-instructor-tips/11-korean-instructor-tips-collected.md` §9 quotes a Korean instructor on exactly this: 한국식 사고로 영작하면 관사와 단복수가 무너진다).
2. **Collocation and register precision.** "consuming tax", "help people rewind", "soothe people's mind" and "well-made paintings" are near-misses that a native reader notices. The topic has a ready set: "subsidise the arts", "allocate funding", "public expenditure", "raise revenue", "preserve cultural heritage" (`contexts/10-task2-topic-banks/topic-16-arts-and-culture.md`; `contexts/10-task2-topic-banks/topic-06-government-and-politics.md`).
3. **Explicit subjects and agreement in long sentences.** "there are people who believes", and "provide them with a lot of money can be regarded as…" — the second needs a gerund subject ("Providing…"), which is the exact structure flagged in `contexts/09-instructor-tips/10-common-korean-chinese-speaker-errors.md` §C1. Both appear in your longest sentences, not your short ones.
4. **Naming what your pronouns point to.** "those issues", "the issues", "the same problems", "them" — none names a referent, and in "provide **them**" the nearest plural nouns are "modern technologies" and "problems" (`contexts/08-vocabulary-grammar/03-cohesion-error-correction-bank.md` §3).
5. **Synthesising the conclusion.** Restate the balance, not just your side (`contexts/05-band-6-to-7/task-response-upgrades.md` §9).
