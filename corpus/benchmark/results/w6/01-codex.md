## Structural feedback

The essay addresses both views and gives a consistent opinion from the introduction through the conclusion. The body paragraphs are reasonably balanced and each has a recognisable point–explanation–example sequence. However, Body 1 explains a benefit of art without establishing why government funding, rather than ticket sales or private support, is necessary; the strongest justification would be that markets may under-supply culturally valuable work (`contexts/10-task2-topic-banks/topic-16-arts-and-culture.md`). Body 2 begins with a relevant finite-budget argument but then jumps to chatbots replacing artists, rather than comparing arts funding with a specific public priority such as healthcare or education. The conclusion restates the technology comparison instead of synthesising the benefit and opportunity cost (`contexts/05-band-6-to-7/task-response-upgrades.md` §9). Because structure already reaches low Band 7, eliminating the recurring language errors is the fastest route to an overall 7.0.

## Per-criterion justification

### Task Response / Achievement — 7.0

You discuss the benefit of supporting artists and the opposing finite-budget view, while maintaining the opinion that public money should be used elsewhere. Both sides receive explanation and an example, satisfying all parts of the task. However, “artists can be replaced by modern technologies” is over-generalised, and the essay never fully explains why the artistic benefit requires public subsidy. This fits the Band 7 allowance for some over-generalisation or lack of precision rather than Band 6’s more substantial underdevelopment (`contexts/01-band-descriptors/task2-band-descriptors.md` §Band 7).

### Coherence and Cohesion — 7.0

The four-paragraph progression is logical: introduction, benefit, opposing argument and conclusion. Devices including “while”, “Even though”, “For instance”, “However”, “Since” and “Despite” show more range than a mechanical numbered sequence. Reference becomes less precise in “those issues”, “the issues” and “they”, while “It is because” creates a slightly awkward connection. These occasional inaccuracies are compatible with Band 7 because the overall argument remains easy to follow (`contexts/01-band-descriptors/task2-band-descriptors.md` §Band 7).

### Lexical Resource — 6.0

There is useful range in “alleviate”, “emotionally attached”, “annual spending” and “the advent of large language models”. Nevertheless, inaccurate combinations occur frequently: “consuming tax”, “spend the tax”, “help people rewind” and “finance them”. “A huge financial support” also mismanages the uncountable noun *support*. This is Band 6: meaning remains clear, but word choice and collocation are not yet accurate enough for Band 7 (`contexts/01-band-descriptors/task2-band-descriptors.md` §Band 6).

### Grammatical Range & Accuracy — 6.0

You attempt relative clauses, concessive clauses and causal structures, as in “artworks, which can potentially alleviate…” and “Despite the fact that…”. Several sentences are error-free, but errors remain frequent in “people who believes”, “a efficient way” and “their works are not the only one”. The final sentence lacks the required gerund subject: “provide them…can be regarded”. Communication is rarely blocked, which supports Band 6, but Band 7 requires more frequent error-free sentences (`contexts/01-band-descriptors/task2-band-descriptors.md` §Band 6).
