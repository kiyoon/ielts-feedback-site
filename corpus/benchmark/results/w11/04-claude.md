## Focus areas

1. **Explicit comparison between the three crime types — especially the 2007 crossover.** This is the documented number-one 6→7 lever for Task 1: "The single biggest jump from 6 to 7 is the overview + grouping move. Band 6 candidates list; Band 7 candidates *select and group*" (`contexts/02-task1-academic/samples-band-7_0.md`). Armed robbery starts as the **highest** line and ends as the **middle** line; assault overtakes it in 2007 and finishes at roughly **1.9 times** its level. You report both lines accurately and never once put them in the same sentence.
2. **The two named clause-control patterns.** "there was a gradual climb …, eventually reached a peak" is the double-predicate-after-*there be* error in `contexts/09-instructor-tips/10-common-korean-chinese-speaker-errors.md` §F1 — "a finite clause can have only one finite verb; add a relative pronoun or convert the second verb to a participle." "stayed relatively stable …, maintained the similar level" is a *different* pattern: Korean topic-drop, §K ("Is important." → "**It** is important."). Two rules, two fixes. These are the recurring family from W2/W3/W5, and grammar is the criterion you scored 7 on in W10.
3. **"The number of" + plural, and *the* vs *a* with *similar*.** "the number of armed robbery" → "armed robber**ies**" (§B1). Note you already get the *verb* right — "the number of X **remained**" is correctly singular (§B3). "the similar level" → "**a** similar level"; §A3 lists *only/main/same/top/first/last* as always taking *the* — *similar* is not on that list.
4. **"On the contrary" → "By contrast."** *On the contrary* contradicts a previous statement; you are contrasting two data sets. `contexts/09-instructor-tips/03-trend-language-bank.md` §12 gives *by contrast / conversely / on the other hand*.
5. **Trend-collocation precision.** Real, but ranked below the others because you do not need LR 7 to reach 7.0.
