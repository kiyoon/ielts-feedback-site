## Structural feedback

## Focus areas

**1. The overview has to be true before it can be clear.** `contexts/01-band-descriptors/task1-academic-band-descriptors.md` notes that "Overview is gating for Band 7+" — but a clear overview that misdescribes the data is worse than a hedged one, because the inaccuracy sits in the most visible sentence you write. Read the three lines twice, once for direction and once for *anything that breaks the direction*, before you write *Overall*. See also `contexts/08-vocabulary-grammar/04-task-response-error-correction-bank.md` §7.

**2. Compare, don't just list.** `contexts/08-vocabulary-grammar/04-task-response-error-correction-bank.md` §8 names this exactly: "No comparison between categories… Compare: 'X was twice as high as Y'." You have three lines that cross over and you say nothing about it. Learn the cross-over bank in `contexts/09-instructor-tips/03-trend-language-bank.md` §9: *overtook / surpassed / the two lines converged / outstripped*. Two comparison sentences would move Task Achievement on their own.

**3. The "number of + plural" frame.** Every essay from W1 to W11 has lost marks here, so this is now your highest-persistence item. In English, *the number of* is always followed by a plural: *the number of robberies*, *the number of murders*. `contexts/09-instructor-tips/10-common-korean-chinese-speaker-errors.md` §B1 is the drill; §A2 covers the *the similar level* → *a similar level* half of the same habit. `contexts/09-instructor-tips/11-korean-instructor-tips-collected.md` §9 gives the Korean-instructor fix: "한국식 사고로 영작하면 관사와 단복수가 무너진다" — write the English noun-phrase skeleton before you write the sentence.

**4. Finite verb versus participle after a comma.** Twice you attach a second action to a sentence with a comma and then use a past-tense verb with no subject: *…, eventually reached a peak* and *…, maintained the similar level*. Korean lets a clause trail like that; English needs either *-ing* (*eventually reaching*), a new subject (*and it reached*), or a preposition (*before reaching*). This is new, and it is the difference between GRA 6 and the GRA 7 you already proved you can hit. `contexts/09-instructor-tips/03-trend-language-bank.md` §12 gives the connective alternatives; §N of `contexts/09-instructor-tips/10-common-korean-chinese-speaker-errors.md` is the audit checklist.

**5. Trend-word precision.** *Showed a plummet* mixes registers (a plummet is not something a figure "shows"); *the mere increase* is not a collocation; *merely 500 cases* misuses *merely* as a quantity marker. `contexts/09-instructor-tips/03-trend-language-bank.md` §1 gives the intensity calibration table and §11 the intensifier modifiers that actually pair with trend nouns — *a sharp decline*, *a modest rebound*, *a marginal rise*.

**Task Achievement — 6.** You do attempt an overview and you do categorise, which is the Band 6 floor cleared. But the overview says *the number of murder remained stable*, and the graph shows murder climbing from about 490 in 2003 to roughly 850 in 2005 — close to double — before falling back. Band 7 requires that "main trends or differences are identified", and a doubling inside a ten-year window is a main feature you have called stable. *Began at just below 3,500 cases in 2003* is also a misreading: 2003 sits near 3,300, and 3,500 is closer to the 2004 peak, so your next sentence (*rose by approximately 250 cases*) implies a peak near 3,750 that does not exist. Finally, the prompt asks you to *make comparisons where relevant* and there is no comparison anywhere between the three crimes. That combination caps you at 6.

**Coherence & Cohesion — 6.** The progression is easy to follow and the signposting is deliberate. But *On the contrary* is the wrong connector: it contradicts a preceding statement, whereas you want to contrast two data sets, which is *By contrast*. *After that* and *Afterwards* appear in adjacent sentences doing the same job. *The figure for* opens three sentences and *can be seen* two, so cohesion becomes mechanical rather than referential — you never use *it*, *this category* or *the same pattern* to bind sentences. And murder is appended to the assault block after *Meanwhile* rather than grouped, so one of your three data sets has no home.

**Lexical Resource — 6.** Your range is real: *plummet*, *dramatically*, *gradual climb*, *maintained*, *relatively stable* are all Task 1 vocabulary and correctly chosen for direction. What holds you at 6 is form and partnership. *Showed a plummet* uses a noun where the verb belongs (*plummeted*). *The mere increase* should be *a modest increase* or *a slight rebound* — *mere* means "nothing more than", not "small". *Maintained the similar level with merely 500 cases* misuses both the article and the preposition; it should be *remained at a similar level of around 500 cases*. Band 7 asks for "awareness of style and collocation", and these are collocation misses rather than range misses — which is good news, because collocation is faster to fix than range.

**Grammatical Range & Accuracy — 6.** You control relative time expressions, passives (*can be seen*), and quantified-change structures, so the range is there. Accuracy is the issue. *How the number of major crimes occurred in London changed* is missing a relative pronoun — it needs *that occurred* or *occurring*. *The number of armed robbery* and *of murder* omit the plural. *Maintained the similar level* uses the wrong article twice. And the two dangling past-tense verbs (*eventually reached a peak*, *maintained the similar level*) leave clauses without subjects. About five of your ten sentences are fully error-free; Band 7 needs "error-free sentences are frequent" and, crucially, needs the errors to be scattered rather than systematic. Yours are systematic — the same article rule and the same clause rule, repeated.
