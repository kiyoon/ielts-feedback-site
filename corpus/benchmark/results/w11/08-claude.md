## Structural feedback

Your four-paragraph shape is right and you should keep it: introduction, overview, one crime described in detail, then the remaining two. Grouping assault and murder into one paragraph is a legitimate choice, not a fault — they share the property of being broadly flat or gently rising while armed robbery collapses. At 185 words you are inside the 160–190 range that `contexts/02-task1-academic/samples-band-7_0.md` gives as the Band 7 target. You also correctly did **not** write a conclusion; `contexts/02-task1-academic/chart-types-overview.md` §10 states plainly that Task 1 has an overview, not a conclusion, and many candidates lose marks by adding one.

What the structure is missing is inside the overview, not around it. Your overview names three directions but never says which crime was highest, which was lowest, or that two of the lines swap places. `contexts/01-band-descriptors/task1-academic-band-descriptors.md` Note 2 makes the overview the gate for Band 7, and Note 4 asks the writer to group features rather than describing each line in isolation.

## Focus areas

1. **Rank the three crimes and name the crossover.** Armed robbery starts highest; assault ends highest; murder is far the lowest throughout. Between 2006 and 2007 assault overtakes armed robbery and never falls behind again. `contexts/08-vocabulary-grammar/04-task-response-error-correction-bank.md` §8 lists "No comparison between categories" as a Task 1 error and gives the repair frame "X was twice as high as Y"; `contexts/09-instructor-tips/03-trend-language-bank.md` §9 supplies *overtook / surpassed / outstripped*.
2. **Trend collocations.** "Maintained the similar level" is not a natural partnership. §4 of the same trend bank gives "level off at X" and asks you always to pair it with the value.
3. **"The number of" takes a plural noun**, and *similar* never takes *the*. `contexts/08-vocabulary-grammar/01-grammar-error-correction-bank.md` §1 row 25 gives exactly this correction; `contexts/09-instructor-tips/10-common-korean-chinese-speaker-errors.md` §A3 lists *only / main / same / top / first / last* as the words that always take *the* — *similar* is deliberately not on that list.
4. **A comma cannot be followed by a bare finite verb.** Add a conjunction or convert the verb to an *-ing* participle. Adding a subject alone would create a comma splice — the error you retired in W9 — so use the participle instead.
5. **Choose connectors by meaning.** "On the contrary" contradicts a statement just made; a plain difference takes "In contrast" or "By contrast" (`contexts/09-instructor-tips/03-trend-language-bank.md` §12).
