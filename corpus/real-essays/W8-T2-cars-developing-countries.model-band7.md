## Prompt

More and more people in developing countries are purchasing cars for the first time. What problems does this cause? What do you think is a possible solution?

## Model answer (Band 7)

It is increasingly common for people in developing countries to buy a car of their own for the first time. This trend brings two serious problems — a rise in road accidents and ever-greater consumption of fossil fuels. Nevertheless, both problems can be eased if governments make public transport a genuinely attractive alternative to driving.

The most immediate danger is a sharp increase in traffic accidents. Most first-time buyers are, by definition, inexperienced drivers, and in many developing countries licensing systems and traffic enforcement are still too weak to prepare them properly. When millions of new motorists join roads that were never designed for such volumes, collisions inevitably become more frequent. Moreover, fossil fuel consumption is projected to climb steeply as car ownership spreads. Unlike renewable sources such as solar or wind power, petrol cannot be produced artificially: fossil fuels take millions of years to form, so reserves consumed at today's pace can never be replaced.

The most realistic solution is to persuade these new buyers to drive less and use public transport more. The arithmetic is simple: a single bus can carry dozens of passengers who would otherwise travel in separate cars, so every full bus means fewer vehicles, less congestion and lower fuel demand. An awareness campaign alone, however, rarely changes behaviour. Governments also need to make buses and subways cheaper, safer and more reliable than driving — by subsidising fares and expanding routes in fast-growing cities — so that leaving the car at home becomes the easier choice rather than a sacrifice.

In conclusion, the arrival of millions of first-time car owners is likely to cost lives on the road and to speed up the depletion of fossil fuels. However, both problems can be contained if governments invest seriously in public transport and then campaign to make using it a normal part of daily life.

## Why this is Band 7 (and how it fixes your version)

- **Both splices are gone — using your own words.** Your intro joined two
  sentences with ", nevertheless," and your conclusion with ", however,". The
  model keeps both adverbs and simply gives each a full stop before it:
  "…fossil fuels**. Nevertheless,** both problems…" / "…fossil fuels**.
  However,** both problems…". This is the entire fix — punctuation, not
  vocabulary.
- **The accident claim is proved with the prompt's own ingredients.** Your
  version said accidents "will increase naturally". The model gives the
  mechanism the prompt hands you: *first-time* buyers are inexperienced
  drivers, and licensing/enforcement in *developing* countries cannot yet cope
  — followed by the consequence (roads never designed for such volumes). No
  invented statistics; just the causal chain.
- **The solution has a behavioural mechanism.** Your campaign is kept — but
  demoted to a supporting move ("An awareness campaign alone, however, rarely
  changes behaviour"), while subsidised fares and expanded routes explain why
  a new car owner would actually switch. This is the §8.4 problem→solution
  mapping your Task Response needed.
- **One sentence now serves both problems.** "Every full bus means fewer
  vehicles, less congestion and lower fuel demand" states the fuel link your
  essay left implicit — and showcases *fewer* + countable next to *less* +
  uncountable, your longest-running error family, used correctly in a single
  clause.
- **The countability family is clean throughout.** *Public transport* (never
  "transports"), *less traffic*, *fewer vehicles*, *the roads* with its
  article. And *carry dozens of passengers* replaces "handle several people at
  once" — vehicles *carry*; *dozens* is the honest scale of a bus.
- **The conclusion answers with a plural referent and adds nothing false.**
  "**Both problems** can be contained…" repairs your ambiguous "it", names the
  two problems in fresh words ("cost lives on the road", "the depletion of
  fossil fuels"), and keeps the campaign — subordinated to the investment that
  makes it credible.
- **Your best sentence pattern is promoted, not replaced.** Your error-free
  "it is projected that fossil fuel consumption will increase" becomes "fossil
  fuel consumption is projected to climb steeply" — the same impersonal
  projection frame you already control, in its tighter raised form.
- **Controlled Band 7 range without showing off.** A fronted *When*-clause, a
  colon introducing an explanation, a *who* relative clause, one correctly
  punctuated mid-sentence *however*, a *so that* purpose clause, and the
  em-dash policy aside — all error-free, none beyond what your W5–W8 range
  already attempts.
