## Model answer (Band 7)

The line graph compares the numbers of armed robberies, assaults and murders recorded in London between 2003 and 2012.

Overall, armed robbery fell substantially, whereas assault increased and became the most common of the three crimes. Murder remained the least common category throughout, despite some fluctuations.

Armed robbery started at approximately 3,400 cases in 2003 and peaked at around 3,700 the following year. The figure then dropped sharply to just over 1,000 in 2008. After recovering to about 1,500 in 2009, it remained close to this level until 2012. This was less than half the initial figure.

Assault cases numbered roughly 2,300 in 2003 and fluctuated before reaching about 2,000 in 2008. They had overtaken armed robbery by 2007. The figure subsequently rose overall, reaching approximately 2,800 in 2012 despite a small dip in 2011. Meanwhile, murder cases fluctuated between roughly 500 and 900, peaking in 2005 before ending the period at about 500, similar to their starting level.

## Why these changes help

- **Task Achievement:** the overview compares directions and rankings. The details identify the crossover by 2007, robbery's substantial net decline, and murder's range and early peak.
- **Coherence:** the original introduction → overview → robbery → assault/murder sequence is retained. Related movements share sentences, and references connect them without a new linker at every step.
- **Lexical Resource:** complete combinations such as *peaked at*, *recovering to* and *fluctuated between* replace awkward trend phrases. Approximation avoids false precision.
- **Grammar:** plural incident nouns follow *the numbers of*. In *the figure subsequently rose overall, reaching…*, the participle clearly attaches to the figure that rose.
