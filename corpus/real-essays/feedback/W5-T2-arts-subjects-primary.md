## Structural feedback

The essay's architecture is the best of the five: a clear agree position, two reasons previewed in the introduction and delivered one per body paragraph, an example in each, and a conclusion — the shape the `contexts/05-band-6-to-7/paragraph-structure-band7.md` skeleton prescribes, at 300 words. Three structural residues keep it from being fully Band-7-polished. First, both topic sentences are formulaic 30–40-word chains ("The primary reason why the school should treat minor subjects as important as other subjects is because…"; "The second main reason why teaching those minor subjects is important especially for elementary school students is that…") — shorter claims would sharpen progression. Second, the K-pop example asserts trainee ages but never states the link to early talent-spotting; one sentence completes the PEEL loop. Third, the conclusion restates rather than synthesises — the recurring W2/W4 pattern (`contexts/05-band-6-to-7/task-response-upgrades.md` §9).

## Per-criterion justification

**TR 7.** The agree/disagree prompt is fully addressed with "a clear and developed position": "This essay totally agree with that statement" through "In conclusion, I strongly believe…" Main ideas are "extended and supported" — talent-discovery is developed via the entrance-exam mechanism and K-pop example, creativity via the specific-answer contrast and the drawing/melodies example. The Band 7 allowance fits the weaknesses: "a tendency to over-generalise" ("everyone has their own unique talents") and "a lack of focus and precision in supporting ideas" (the K-pop link is implicit). This is not Band 6, whose marker is conclusions "unclear, unjustified or repetitive" in position terms and ideas "insufficiently developed" — neither applies.

**CC 7.** Paragraphing is logical with one central topic each and "clear progression throughout"; devices span "Moreover," "For example," "For instance," "whereas," and referential "This will help the pupils…" The imperfections — formulaic topic sentences, "those minor subjects" repetition — are Band 7's "some inaccuracies or some over/under use," not Band 6's "faulty or mechanical" cohesion with illogical paragraphing. Calibration: the IELTS Advantage report in `contexts/06-feedback-patterns/model-feedback-examples.md` awards CC 7 to an essay while noting "a tendency to overuse" devices, and `contexts/04-task2-essays/samples-band-7_0.md` §Sample 4 holds CC 7 with comparable repetition. I conceded this from my original 6 in Panel 04; the descriptor fit and anchors support codex's call.

**LR 6.** There is adequate range ("potential talents," "foster their creativity," "pupils," "unconventional") but "a rather restricted range or a lack of precision in word choice": "minor subjects" repeated four times, "totally agree," "figuring out their skills," the vague "millions of unique mdelodies and scenarios" (with the spelling slip). Band 7 requires "an awareness of style and collocation is evident" — the calques and register drops are too frequent for that yet.

**GRA 6.** Complex structures are attempted and sometimes succeed (the "whereas" contrast), but "more complex structures are not marked by the same level of accuracy as in simple structures": "This essay totally agree" (agreement), the "…and eventually, cannot develop" subject-drop collapse, "when they are teenager," "are equally important as." Error-free sentences are not yet "frequent" across the complex attempts, which is the Band 7 gate.

## Before your next essay (added after the panel, from its agreed priorities)

Correcting this essay is the smaller half of the value — these forward steps are what actually move the bands:

- **Drill 1 — articles on generic nouns (15 min).** Read §A2 of `contexts/09-instructor-tips/10-common-korean-chinese-speaker-errors.md`, then rewrite five sentences from your own W1–W5 essays that use "the school / the students / the parents"-style generic reference with bare plurals. Corrections you generate yourself stick far better than corrections you read.
- **Drill 2 — subjects survive to the end of the sentence (10 min).** Take the three sentences where a clause collapsed (W2's comma splice, W3's "Although their logic could not fully explain", W5's "…and eventually, cannot develop their talents") and rewrite each so every verb has an explicit subject. Then write two new long sentences of your own and run the same check.
- **Exam proofread routine (last 4 minutes, every essay).** Pass 1: every generic noun → bare plural, no "the". Pass 2: after every "and / so / which", check the verb still has its subject. Pass 3: ban-list scan — "the reason … is because", "a lot of", "totally", and any label repeated 3+ times.
- **Stretch goals for W6 — attempt, don't just avoid.** Use one "the more …, the more …" comparative, one "By + -ing" sentence opener, and rotate your key noun phrase at least three ways ("arts subjects → these disciplines → such classes"). Band 7 GRA/LR reward attempted range that mostly works, and none of these structures appear anywhere in W1–W5.
- **Retirement test.** If W6 shows zero generic-article slips and no dropped subjects, GRA very likely flips 6 → 7 — and that single flip makes the task score 7.0. That is the whole remaining gap.
