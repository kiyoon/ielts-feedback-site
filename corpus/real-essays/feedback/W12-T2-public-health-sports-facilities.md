## Structural feedback

**Keep the four-paragraph plan. Give each body a distinct job.** Your first body already has a sensible mechanism: a shorter journey makes participation easier. Complete it with regular exercise → improved fitness, showing why supporters consider facilities the best approach. You may qualify the view at the end, but the long objection currently does much of Body 2's work in advance. Move it to the transition and use the space to finish the positive case (`contexts/05-band-6-to-7/paragraph-structure-band7.md` §4.2).

**Rebuild Body 2 around one named alternative.** “There are various ways to improve individuals' health” announces a list you never supply. The table-tennis/yoga contrast mainly illustrates different preferences; yoga classes can also use sports facilities. A clear alternative is nutrition education: lessons about everyday choices → practical changes to drinks or meals → better diets, including among children who rarely play sport. This is a proposed example, not a claim about an existing Korean programme (`contexts/05-band-6-to-7/task-response-upgrades.md` §5).

The logical distinction is **not enough ≠ negligible benefit**. A measure can help many people without helping everyone. Your argument establishes that facilities alone may miss some people; it does not yet establish that their population-wide benefit is marginal. Keep your position that other measures are needed, and make the conclusion bring that limitation together with the access benefit. Its 23-word length is not itself a fault (`contexts/05-band-6-to-7/task-response-upgrades.md` §9).

However, the first body's closing objection becomes the next body's opening claim; “everyone is different” then repeats the same idea without adding a concrete policy. Meaning is clear, but the combinations are not consistently precise. Repeating essential terms is acceptable. *A lot of* can be tightened to *many*, and *it's* expanded to *it is* for formality, but these are secondary edits, not automatic band caps (`contexts/01-band-descriptors/task2-band-descriptors.md` §Band 6).

### Grammatical Range & Accuracy — 6.0

You attempt causal, relative and concessive clauses and use passives successfully. Control is less consistent in “some people especially who”, verbal “access to” twice, and “find yoga class”. The opening's modal sequence also reads less cleanly than a direct statement about building facilities. Assessing these patterns across the essay supports 6; there is no need to invent an error-free-sentence threshold. In “effect to improve”, *to improve* is an infinitive, so it is not the same preposition error as “effect to people”. The phrase still benefits from a more natural rewrite (`contexts/01-band-descriptors/task2-band-descriptors.md` §Band 6).
