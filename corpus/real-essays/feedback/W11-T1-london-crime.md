## Structural feedback

Keep the information sequence: introduction → overview → armed robbery → assault and murder. Grouping the last two categories together is reasonable; murder does not require its own paragraph. Your overview already contrasts directions. The most useful content repair is to extend those comparisons to rankings: armed robbery fell sharply, assault became the most common crime, and murder remained the least common despite fluctuations. In the details, report that assault had overtaken armed robbery by 2007 and give murder's 2005 peak. Your present account devotes more space to successive steps than to these comparisons. Redistribute that space rather than adding a long conclusion or explanations of why crime changed (`contexts/02-task1-academic/chart-types-overview.md` §2; `contexts/08-vocabulary-grammar/04-task-response-error-correction-bank.md` §8).

## Per-criterion justification

### Task Achievement — 6.0

You identify the major decline in armed robbery and the overall increase in assault, then support the account with figures. Most numerical estimates are reasonable approximations. However, the account leaves the changing ranking implicit and does not identify murder as consistently the least common category. `stayed relatively stable` compresses similar endpoints and intervening fluctuations into one claim, without illustrating the early peak of roughly 900 cases. Taken together, these omissions fit adequate highlighting at Band 6 more securely than Band 7's clear selection and illustration of main features. No single omitted crossover is an automatic band cap (`contexts/01-band-descriptors/task1-academic-band-descriptors.md` §Band 6).

### Coherence and Cohesion — 6.0

The information progresses clearly and the references are generally recoverable. The weakness is the repeated step-by-step presentation through `Then`, `After that` and `Afterwards`, combined with an inappropriate contrast marker. `On the contrary` normally rejects a preceding claim; here you are comparing different series. CC is close to 7, but the overall pattern is still more mechanical than flexible. This is not a ban on chronological reporting, a linker quota or a requirement for a separate murder paragraph (`contexts/01-band-descriptors/task1-academic-band-descriptors.md` §Band 6).

### Lexical Resource — 6.0

`decrease dramatically`, `a gradual climb` and `reached a peak` show useful range. Precision is less consistent in `showed a plummet`, `the mere increase` and the repeated stability expression. *Mere/merely* introduces a minimising judgment that adds little to this factual report; *merely* is not grammatically forbidden before a number. *Plummet* also has a noun sense meaning an abrupt drop ([Merriam-Webster](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/plummet)); the useful correction here is the more natural combination *fell sharply*, not a rule that the noun cannot exist. Accurate familiar combinations would improve this response more than rarer synonyms (`contexts/01-band-descriptors/task1-academic-band-descriptors.md` §Band 6).

### Grammatical Range & Accuracy — 6.0

The response attempts an embedded question, a *while* clause, coordination and passives. The introduction needs a relative marker in `crimes occurred in London changed`; the later `eventually reached` and final `maintained` are not connected grammatically to the preceding clauses. Noun-number errors and `the similar level` also recur. Meaning remains clear, but complex-sentence control is less secure than in W10. This is a judgment across the response, not a fixed error-free-sentence percentage or a claim that one participle correction alone guarantees 7 (`contexts/01-band-descriptors/task1-academic-band-descriptors.md` §Band 6).
