## Structural feedback

The four-paragraph problem→solution architecture is right, and it is the
correct shape for this prompt type
(`contexts/05-band-6-to-7/task-response-upgrades.md` §8.4): both problems in
one paragraph, the solution in the other, both prompt questions answered. Four
repairs, in value order. First, **the accident problem is asserted, not
proved**: "if people buy more cars, there will be more traffic, and thus, car
accidents will increase naturally" is circular — the prompt hands you the
mechanism (millions of *first-time* drivers, licensing and enforcement systems
in *developing* countries that cannot yet cope) and the essay never touches
it. Second, **the solution has no behavioural mechanism**: a campaign is
named, but nothing explains why a new car owner would respond to it — cheaper
fares, expanded routes and reliability would carry that load, and §8.4's rule
is that each solution must map onto a problem you raised. Third, **the
conclusion shrinks your own answer**: the essay's solution is public
transport, the conclusion keeps only "conducting a public awareness campaign"
— the means, not the solution — and repeats the introduction's wording
otherwise. Fourth, a small roadmap slip: the introduction lists fuel before
accidents; Body 1 treats accidents first. Match the orders. The single
highest-impact fix is rebuilding Body 2 as policy → a practical reason to
switch → fewer accidents and lower fuel demand; that one paragraph is where
TR 7 lives. Keep the fossil-fuel chain — finite supply, slow formation,
eventual depletion — which the panel called the most developed reasoning in
the essay.

### Coherence and Cohesion — 6.0

### Lexical Resource — 6.0
