## Structural feedback

The four-paragraph shape is right and the view-per-paragraph split is exactly
what W3's feedback asked for. Three problems remain, and the first is the one
worth your time.

**The conclusion gives no verdict.** It concedes the teacher case and then
trails into a subordinate observation. One added clause fixes the highest-value
defect in the essay. This is worth more than every lexical upgrade in the table
below.

**Body 2 repeats Body 1 instead of developing its own idea.** This is the
correction to the earlier review: the paragraph is not too short, it is
*redundant*. It opens on parental knowledge — the right idea — and then ends on
teacher overwhelm, which is Body 1's point. Two body paragraphs that say the
same thing is a named fault: each body needs a distinct main idea
(`contexts/08-vocabulary-grammar/04-task-response-error-correction-bank.md` §3
row 8). Your closing sentence should say what a *parent* does, not what a
teacher cannot. Your personal commentary belongs in the closing link sentence
(`contexts/05-band-6-to-7/paragraph-structure-band7.md` §4.2).

**Your best evidence proves the wrong claim.** The Korean certification route —
education degree, bachelor's, national examination — is genuinely specific
evidence, the best in your corpus. But it shows teachers are academically
qualified. It does not show they are better at teaching a child to apologise,
share or compromise. You then conclude they "can teach and guide children how to
be a good member of society better than their parents," which the example does
not establish. Keep the example; state what it actually shows.

One smaller drift: "Some might have mental health issues or even a rare disease"
moves from social behaviour to medical conditions. The idea underneath is sound
and worth keeping — every child has a different temperament and needs a
different response.

## Per-criterion justification

### Task Response / Achievement — 6.0

Both views are addressed and a position is presented, so the Band 6 gate is met.
Band 7 fails on two descriptor points. Band 7 requires "a clear and developed
position… unambiguous from intro through to conclusion"; your conclusion states
none, which is Band 6's "the conclusions drawn may be unclear, unjustified or
repetitive." Band 7 also requires main ideas "extended and supported": Body 2
ends by repeating Body 1 rather than developing the parental case, and the
school side's example does not prove the claim attached to it — Band 6's "some
supporting arguments and evidence may be less relevant or inadequate." Codex
initially argued TR 7 on the strength of the introduction and the two-view
split, then accepted that the missing verdict is decisive.

### Coherence and Cohesion — 6.0

Paragraphing is logical and progression is clear — Band 7 territory, and the
essay would hold CC 7 on paragraphing alone. Reference and sentence-level
cohesion pull it down. Band 7 tolerates "a few lapses… but these are minor";
here there are four and they are not minor: "handle that issue" (which issue?),
"they have to take care of" (no antecedent — it follows singular *a homeroom
teacher*), "it is easy to get outnumbered" (agency hidden, in the conclusion),
and the comma splice at "more than anyone else, thus, they should know". Band 6:
"the use of reference and substitution may lack flexibility or clarity" plus
"cohesion within… sentences may be faulty."

### Lexical Resource — 6.0

Range is adequate and you take risks — "undeniable", "inclined to this view",
"traits and personalities", "overwhelmed" are all appropriate. Band 6's
risk-taker clause fits exactly: "there will be a wider range of vocabulary used
but higher degrees of inaccuracy or inappropriacy." *Juveniles* for
schoolchildren is a false friend with a legal connotation; *efficiently* stands
where *effectively* is meant; *thanks to*, *nice adults*, *take care of*, *so
many* and *handle that issue* are spoken register; and "cirtification" and
"bachlor" are spelling errors — Band 6 allows "some errors in spelling and/or
word formation", Band 7 only "a few… they do not detract from overall clarity".

### Grammatical Range & Accuracy — 6.0

Range is real: relative clauses, concession ("even though", "despite the fact
that"), causal subordination, comparatives, and a correctly handled *the number
of students*. Accuracy under that load is not. "Some would argued" is a modal
error in the opening five words; "experts who has cirtification" is agreement in
a relative clause — the same fault as W6's "people who believes"; "every
children" is number; "they had observed" should be present perfect; "as a
homeroom teacher, it is challenging" leaves the introductory phrase attached to
nothing. Error-free sentences are not yet "frequent". Codex's opening GRA 5 was
withdrawn as too harsh — the errors rarely impede communication, which keeps
this at Band 6 — but it is the weakest of the four.
