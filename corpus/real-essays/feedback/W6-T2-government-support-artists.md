## Structural feedback

The four-paragraph structure works, and both views receive their own four-sentence body paragraph, although the paragraphs are not equal in length. Body 1 develops art’s emotional benefit through explanation and examples, but it does not quite explain why taxpayers rather than consumers or private sponsors should fund that benefit. Body 2 begins circularly—support is wasteful “because it is a waste of money”—before supplying the real finite-budget and chatbot reasons. Your conclusion genuinely weighs both sides through “while artists…help people,” but its final sentence makes a broader judgment than the evidence supports. The highest-impact structural repair is to replace two sentences: add a funding → public access → social benefit link in Body 1, and give Body 2 a non-circular opening reason. Keep the digital-technology position; it needs clearer cost, reach or accessibility evidence, not replacement with healthcare or education.

## Per-criterion justification

### Task Response / Achievement — 7.0

Both views are appropriately addressed: Body 1 explains how art may improve emotional well-being, while Body 2 uses limited budgets and chatbots to support redirecting funding. Your opinion is explicit and consistent throughout. The public-funding justification remains implicit, and “artists can be replaced by modern technologies” is too sweeping for one chatbot example. However, these are problems of precision and over-generalisation rather than a missing or merely listed position, which best fits Band 7 (`contexts/01-band-descriptors/task2-band-descriptors.md` §Band 7).

### Coherence and Cohesion — 7.0

The argument progresses logically through introduction, two distinct views and a conclusion. “Some…others,” “For instance,” “However,” “Since” and “Despite” express several relationships rather than mechanically numbering ideas. “Those issues” refers locally to mental-health issues in Body 1, while “the issues” follows stress in Body 2; neither crosses a paragraph boundary. Final “them” is genuinely ambiguous because *artists*, *works*, *people*, *technologies* and *problems* provide competing nearby plural referents, but this limited inaccuracy is compatible with Band 7.

### Lexical Resource — 6.0

There is useful range in “alleviate,” “annual spending” and “the advent of large language models.” Nevertheless, inaccurate combinations occur frequently: “consuming tax,” “spend the tax,” “finance them” and “help people rewind.” “From the perspective of sociology” also promises an analysis the paragraph does not provide. Meaning remains clear, but collocational precision is not sufficiently consistent for Band 7 (`contexts/01-band-descriptors/task2-band-descriptors.md` §Band 6).

### Grammatical Range & Accuracy — 6.0

You attempt relative, concessive, causal and passive constructions, so range is adequate. Recurring errors remain in “people who believes,” “a efficient way,” “their works are not the only one” and “the society.” The conclusion also needs a gerund clause as its subject: “Providing artists…” rather than “provide them…”. Errors occur in too many sentences for Band 7’s requirement that error-free sentences be frequent (`contexts/01-band-descriptors/task2-band-descriptors.md` §Band 6).
