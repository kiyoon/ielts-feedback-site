## Structural feedback

The four-paragraph architecture is right, the bodies are well balanced (116 / 104 words —
much better than W4, the same question type), and your position is visible from the first
paragraph. Four repairs, in value order.

**First, and worth more than everything else combined: Body 2 argues about the wrong
thing.** The prompt's trade-off is *you hand over personal data, you get access to
software*. Body 1 engages that trade-off. Body 2 does not — it argues that AI is useful
and cheap, which nobody disputes and which would be **equally true if the software
collected no data at all**. That is the scope test in
`contexts/05-band-6-to-7/task-response-upgrades.md` §3: if a paragraph would survive
unchanged under a different prompt, it is not answering this one
(`contexts/05-band-6-to-7/band-6-common-errors.md` §D4;
`contexts/08-vocabulary-grammar/04-task-response-error-correction-bank.md` §3). The fix is
one clause, not a new paragraph — name the exchange in the topic sentence and again in the
link sentence.

**Second, the conclusion changes the question.** Your introduction promises
`the benefits outweigh the wbacks`; your conclusion decides that
`not utilizing modern technology is a more significant downside`. That is a comparison
between *using and not using technology* — not between the advantages and disadvantages of
the exchange. An "outweigh" prompt requires the verdict on the prompt's own terms
(`contexts/08-vocabulary-grammar/04-task-response-error-correction-bank.md` §1, §5).

**Third, Body 1's rebuttal is a real argument that stops one clause short.**
`there is always a risk to ge stolen even though individuals choose not to use the sevice`
is genuinely sophisticated — you are attacking a disadvantage you yourself raised. But it
proves the disadvantage is *unavoidable*, not that the advantage is *larger*; and
`every personal infmation is already stored in digital form` is too absolute — handing data
to one more company is one more point of exposure. Qualify it, then add the weighing clause.

**Fourth, delete `ortdra`.** A stray fragment as the final word of the essay is the last
thing an examiner reads.
