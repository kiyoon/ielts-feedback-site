## Prompt

Some people think that parents should teach their children how to be good members of society. Others, however, believe that school is the best place to learn this. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

## Model answer (Band 7)

Opinion is divided over whether the responsibility for teaching children to behave well in society rests primarily with their families or with their schools. Although classroom life clearly offers valuable practice, I would argue that parents remain better placed to shape social behaviour, because they can adapt their guidance to the individual child.

There is a strong case for the school. Teachers are trained professionals, and a classroom exposes children to the situations in which social skills are actually used: sharing equipment, taking turns in discussion, and apologising after a dispute. In Korea, for example, prospective teachers must complete a degree at a college of education and pass a certification examination, so they enter the profession with formal training that most parents have not received. Where a family has little time or confidence, this daily supervised practice may be the main place a child learns to cooperate.

Even so, professional training cannot substitute for individual knowledge. A homeroom teacher is responsible for a whole class and may know each child for only a single school year, whereas parents observe the same child from infancy and can read the temperament behind a particular behaviour. A child who withdraws from group work may be shy, anxious or simply tired; a parent who knows which it is can respond appropriately, while a teacher managing a large class must apply one rule to everyone. Because honesty and consideration are absorbed most readily from people a child trusts and sees daily, the home retains the stronger influence.

In conclusion, schools give children the daily practice that turns social rules into habits, and trained teachers manage that practice skilfully. Nevertheless, because parents know the individual child and can adjust their guidance accordingly, I believe the primary responsibility still lies with the family, with schools reinforcing rather than replacing what is taught at home.

## Why this is Band 7 (and how it fixes your version)

- **The position survives to the end.** The introduction says parents are
  "better placed to shape social behaviour"; the conclusion says "the primary
  responsibility still lies with the family." This is the Band 7 gate your essay
  missed — your conclusion named no side at all.
- **Your evidence proves its own claim.** The Korea certification route is kept
  verbatim in substance, but it now supports "teachers enter the profession
  with formal training that most parents have not received" — which is what a
  degree and a certification exam actually demonstrate. Your original leap, that
  this makes teachers better than parents at socialisation, is gone.
- **Your own side now develops a distinct idea.** Your Body 2 was never too
  short — at 88 words it sits inside the 80–110 target. The fault was that it
  opened on parental knowledge and *ended on teacher overwhelm*, repeating Body
  1. Here it runs mechanism (a whole class, a single school year, versus
  observation from infancy) → concrete case (the child who withdraws from group
  work) → parent-specific conclusion, and never returns to the teachers. This is
  Band 7's "extended and supported."
- **Every referent points to one visible noun.** *This daily supervised
  practice*, *each of them*, *which it is*, *that practice* — no empty *it*, no
  orphan *they*, no vague *that issue*.
- **No comma splices.** *Whereas*, *while* and a semicolon carry the contrasts
  that "…, thus, they should know…" was trying to carry.
- **Register is consistent.** *Thanks to* is gone — the concessions are carried
  by *Although* and *Even so*. *Pupils* and *children* replace *juveniles*;
  *responsible for a whole class* replaces *take care of*; *respond
  appropriately* replaces *deal with these factors*; *skilfully* replaces *nice*.
- **Prepositions and forms are fixed.** *Responsible for a whole class*;
  *complete a degree at a college of education* rather than *graduate ∅ the
  school of education*; *a certification examination* rather than
  *cirtification*. The *educate someone how to* pattern — which survived from W3
  to W7 — is avoided altogether: the model writes *teaching children to behave
  well in society*, the infinitive complement that *teach* actually takes.
- **Controlled Band 7 range without showing off.** A fronted concessive
  ("Although classroom life…"), a fronted *Where*-clause, a *whereas* contrast, a
  semicolon, two defining relative clauses, a reduced participle ("a teacher
  managing a large class") and a fronted *Because*-clause — all error-free.
