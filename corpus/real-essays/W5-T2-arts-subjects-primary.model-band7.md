## Prompt

Some say that music, art and drama are as important as other school subjects, especially at the primary level. Do you agree or disagree?

## Model answer (Band 7)

It is sometimes claimed that creative subjects such as music, art and drama deserve the same status as core subjects, particularly at primary school. I fully agree with this view, because early exposure to the arts helps children discover their hidden talents and trains the kind of open-ended thinking that single-answer subjects rarely exercise.

Schools should value arts subjects just as highly as academic ones chiefly because every child has a unique talent, and the earlier it is spotted, the more room it has to grow. Once students reach secondary school, entrance exams begin to dominate their timetables, so they lose the chance to explore these disciplines and may never fully develop their abilities. For example, since many K-pop artists begin systematic training in their early teens, a child whose musical talent is noticed in a primary school music class stands a far better chance of being ready in time. Primary school is therefore the only stage at which every child, not just a lucky few, gets that opportunity.

A further reason for taking the arts seriously is that they teach children to think in more inventive ways. Most academic subjects, such as mathematics or science, lead to a single correct answer, whereas creative subjects do not: a portrait can be drawn in countless styles, and a melody can be composed in millions of ways. Pupils therefore learn that some problems have many good solutions, and this habit of mind later enriches even their academic work.

In conclusion, because talent surfaces early and creativity cannot be learned from an answer key, I believe primary schools should give music, art and drama just as much weight as any core subject.

## Why this is Band 7 (and how it fixes the original)

- **Register and label rotation** — "minor subjects" (used four times in the
  original, undercutting its own thesis) becomes a rotating chain: creative
  subjects → arts subjects → these disciplines → the arts. Fixes the panel's
  #1 focus area (collocation/register) and lifts CC via substitution.
- **Subjects survive to the end of every sentence** — the original's
  "…and eventually, cannot develop their talents" collapse (Korean topic-drop)
  is rebuilt as "so **they** lose the chance … and **may never** fully
  develop their abilities". Focus area #2.
- **Bare plurals for generic nouns** — schools, students, pupils, entrance
  exams; no generic "the school / the students", no "university's entrance
  exam". Focus area #3, recurring since W3.
- **PEEL completed** — the K-pop example now names the missing link (talent
  "noticed in a primary school music class") and each body closes with a
  link-back sentence; the original's examples stopped before connecting to
  the claim (flagged in every essay W1–W5).
- **Fixed equative comparison** — "just as much weight as any core subject"
  replaces the fused "equally important as".
- **Synthesis conclusion** — compresses both body arguments into fresh
  wording ("talent surfaces early", "an answer key") instead of repeating the
  introduction, the recurring W2/W4 conclusion problem.
- **Stretch structures demonstrated** — one "the earlier …, the more …"
  correlative, passives in the example pair ("can be drawn", "can be
  composed"), a defining relative ("whose musical talent…") — all absent
  from W1–W5, all safe Band-7 range.
